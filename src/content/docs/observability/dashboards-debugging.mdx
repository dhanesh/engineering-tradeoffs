## Overview

The pillars produce telemetry; **dashboards and the debugging workflow are how a human turns that
telemetry into a root cause.** A wall of fifty graphs is not observability — it's a graveyard nobody
reads during an incident. A *good* dashboard is designed for a path: it answers "is anything wrong?"
at a glance, then lets you **drill down** from that high-level symptom toward the cause, ideally
crossing from one pillar to the next without losing context. This page is about that craft: laying
out dashboards **overview-first then drill-down**, **correlating** metrics, logs, and traces so they
form one investigation rather than three tabs, using **exemplars** to jump from a metric spike
straight to a representative trace, running the **observability-driven debugging loop**, building
**SLO dashboards** that show budget burn, and — because all of this costs money — managing **cost**
through sampling, retention, and cardinality. It's the payoff page: where the three pillars and the
correlation thread from the rest of the cluster become an actual workflow.

## Mental model

**A dashboard is a funnel, not a poster.** The structure that works mirrors how you actually
investigate: the *top* is a small set of overview signals — the golden signals or SLO status for the
whole service — answering one question, "is the user being hurt right now?" If that's green, you
stop. If it's red, you *drill down*: from the service overview to the failing endpoint, from the
endpoint to the slow dependency, from the dependency to the specific host or trace. Each level
narrows the search space. The anti-pattern is the flat "junk drawer" dashboard — every metric anyone
ever wanted, no hierarchy — which forces you to scan everything and find nothing. Design top-down:
what tells me there's a problem, then what tells me where, then what tells me why.

Second model: **the value is in the *seams between* the pillars, not the pillars themselves.** Any
one pillar in isolation leaves you manually pivoting — see a latency spike on a metric, then go to
another tool and guess timestamps to find matching logs, then a third tool to find a trace. The
modern workflow collapses those pivots into clicks via shared identifiers. The keystone is the
**exemplar**: a sampled metric data point that carries a `trace_id`, so you can click the exact spike
on a latency histogram and land on a *representative trace* of a slow request — no timestamp
guessing. From the trace's slow span, the span's `trace_id` joins you to its **logs**. The debugging
loop is therefore: *metric* (something is wrong) → *exemplar* → *trace* (which span is slow) → *log*
(why). Observability is good exactly to the degree those transitions are one click, not three tools.

## Types / Variants

### Dashboard layers (overview → drill-down)

- **Overview / health** — the few golden-signal or SLO panels for the whole service. The "is it
  broken?" screen, glanceable, ideally one row. This is what's on the wall.
- **Service / drill-down** — per-endpoint, per-dependency breakdowns: which route is erroring, which
  downstream call is slow. The "where?" screen.
- **Resource / diagnostic** — USE-style per-resource panels (CPU, memory, connection pools, queue
  depth) and per-host detail. The "why, mechanically?" screen — cause signals you reach *after* a
  symptom sent you here.
- **SLO dashboard** — a dedicated view of each SLI against its target, **error budget remaining**,
  and **burn rate** over multiple windows. Answers "how much budget is left and how fast are we
  spending it?" — the decision surface from the Reliability cluster, made visible.

### Correlation mechanisms

- **Exemplars** — the jump from a metric data point to a trace (via an attached `trace_id`). Turns a
  graph spike into a clickable example.
- **Shared `trace_id`** — threaded through logs and spans, lets one identifier pivot across all three
  pillars.
- **Linked panels / data-link templating** — dashboard links that carry the current filters
  (service, time range, label) from a metrics panel into a logs or traces query, preserving context.

### Cost-management levers

The same three dials recur because cost is the binding constraint:

- **Sampling** — keep a representative fraction of traces (and high-volume boring logs); bias toward
  errors and slow requests (tail sampling) so you keep what you'd investigate.
- **Retention** — tier storage: hot/queryable for days, cheap cold/archived for compliance reach,
  expired beyond that. Retention cost grows linearly with the window.
- **Cardinality budget** — cap distinct metric label combinations; keep high-cardinality dimensions
  on logs/traces (where they belong) and off metric labels (where they explode). The most common
  surprise on the bill.

## When to use / When NOT

- **Design overview-first when:** building any service dashboard — start with golden signals/SLO, add
  drill-down beneath. **Avoid the flat junk-drawer dashboard** — fifty ungrouped panels with no
  hierarchy slow you down in an incident.
- **Use exemplars when:** your stack supports them (Prometheus + a trace backend) — they're the
  single biggest workflow accelerator. **Don't rely on timestamp-guessing** to cross from metrics to
  traces when exemplars can make it a click.
- **Build an SLO dashboard when:** you have SLOs — show budget remaining and burn rate, not just raw
  latency. **Don't substitute** a raw-metrics dashboard for it; budget framing is what drives the
  ship/no-ship decision.
- **Sample and cap retention when:** telemetry volume drives real cost — almost always at scale.
  **Don't sample errors or audit events, and don't retain everything forever** — both are expensive
  in opposite directions.
- **Watch cardinality when:** adding any metric label. **Never** put unbounded IDs on metric labels —
  push that detail to logs/traces.
- **Correlate the pillars when:** debugging anything cross-cutting — wire the data-links once so the
  pivots are clicks. **Don't run three disconnected tools** and join by hand under incident
  pressure.

## Tradeoffs

Dashboard and cost choices trade investigative speed against clutter and spend; the correlation seams
are pure upside if your stack supports them.

| Decision | Buys you | Costs / risk |
| -------- | -------- | ------------ |
| Overview-first, layered dashboard | Fast triage, guided drill-down | Up-front design effort and discipline |
| Flat junk-drawer dashboard | Everything is *somewhere* | Nothing is findable fast; useless in an incident |
| Exemplars + correlation links | Metric → trace → log in clicks | Requires compatible stack + wiring |
| Aggressive sampling | Lower trace/log cost | Risk of missing a rare event (bias toward errors) |
| Long retention | Deep forensic/compliance reach | Linear storage cost growth |
| High-cardinality metric labels | Fine-grained metric slicing | Series explosion — can take the backend down |
| SLO dashboard (budget + burn) | Decision-grade reliability view | Needs SLOs defined and burn-rate math wired |

The cross-cutting tradeoff is the cluster's recurring one — **fidelity versus cost** — now applied at
the *system* level: a dashboard that's too rich is unreadable, telemetry that's too complete is
unaffordable, and the craft is keeping *just enough* visible and stored to drive decisions and corner
unknown-unknowns. Overview-first design solves the readability half; sampling, retention, and
cardinality budgets solve the cost half.

## Diagram

The observability-driven debugging loop: how a single incident flows from a red overview panel down
through the correlated pillars to a root cause.

<Mermaid code={`graph TD
  OV[Overview dashboard<br/>golden signals / SLO status] -->|symptom: latency or errors spike| DRILL[Drill-down panel<br/>which endpoint / dependency?]
  DRILL -->|click exemplar on the spike| TR[Representative trace<br/>via attached trace_id]
  TR -->|read the waterfall| SPAN[Slowest span on the critical path]
  SPAN -->|join by trace_id| LOG[That span's structured logs]
  LOG --> ROOT[Root cause]
  DRILL -.->|resource view| RES[USE panels: CPU, pool, queue]
  RES -.-> ROOT
  ROOT --> FIX[Mitigate, then fix + add a signal so next time it pages]`} />

A red overview panel sends you to the drill-down view to localize the failing endpoint. An exemplar on
the spike jumps you to a representative trace; the waterfall reveals the slow span on the critical
path; the span's `trace_id` joins to its logs, which explain why. Resource (USE) panels corroborate.
The loop ends not just at a fix but at *adding the signal* that would have caught it sooner.

## Real-world examples

- **Grafana** is the de facto dashboarding layer; its **exemplar** support links a Prometheus metric
  point directly to a **Tempo** trace, and data-links carry context into **Loki** logs — the
  three-pillar correlation workflow on this page, productized.
- **Google SRE** dashboard guidance (overview-first, golden-signal-led, drill-down beneath) and the
  *Art of SLOs* materials are the source of the layered-dashboard and SLO-dashboard patterns here.
- **Honeycomb** built its product around the high-cardinality drill-down loop — start from an
  aggregate, then slice by arbitrary dimensions (user, build, region) to corner an unknown-unknown —
  the clearest example of debugging-as-iterative-querying rather than pre-built panels.
- **Datadog, New Relic, and Grafana Cloud** ship unified dashboards with built-in exemplar/trace
  correlation and managed sampling and retention controls — and bill on ingested volume and active
  time series, which is exactly why cardinality and retention budgeting is a first-class operational
  concern.
