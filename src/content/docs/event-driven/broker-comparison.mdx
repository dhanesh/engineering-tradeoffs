## Overview

"Which broker?" is the decision teams agonize over and most often get wrong by
choosing on popularity rather than fit. The honest answer starts one level up: first
decide the **type** you need — queue, log, or pub/sub (see *Message brokers*) — then
the product almost picks itself, modulated by whether you want managed-vs-self-hosted,
your throughput, and your replay needs. Kafka and RabbitMQ are not competitors so much
as different tools: Kafka is a *log*, RabbitMQ is a *queue/router*. Pitting them
head-to-head is usually a category error.

This page profiles the seven brokers most teams actually choose between —
**Kafka, RabbitMQ, AWS SQS/SNS, NATS (+ JetStream), Apache Pulsar, Redis Streams, and
Google Pub/Sub** — on model, ordering, delivery guarantees, throughput,
retention/replay, operational cost, and sweet spot. The goal is to make the pick
defensible: not "everyone uses Kafka," but "we need replay and multiple consumer
groups at high throughput and we can operate it, so Kafka."

## Mental model

Sort the field on two axes and most of the decision falls out.

**Axis 1 — type (what survives after delivery).**
- *Queue/router* (delete on ack): RabbitMQ, SQS.
- *Log* (retain + replay): Kafka, Pulsar, Kinesis, Redis Streams, JetStream.
- *Pub/sub* (fan-out): SNS, NATS core, Google Pub/Sub.
(Several products straddle: Pulsar is a log that *also* does queue semantics; JetStream
is a log bolted onto NATS pub/sub; Google Pub/Sub is durable pub/sub with replay.)

**Axis 2 — who operates it.**
- *Fully managed* (no servers): SQS, SNS, Google Pub/Sub, Kinesis, and managed Kafka
  (MSK, Confluent Cloud) / managed RabbitMQ.
- *You run it* (max control, max ops): self-hosted Kafka, RabbitMQ, Pulsar, NATS,
  Redis.

The single biggest cost driver in practice is **operational burden**, not features.
Self-hosted Kafka and Pulsar are powerful and operationally heavy (ZooKeeper/KRaft,
brokers, partitions, rebalancing; Pulsar adds BookKeeper). Managed cloud-native
services (SQS/SNS, Google Pub/Sub) are nearly zero-ops but lock you to a cloud and cap
some control. NATS is the lightest thing that still does durable streaming.

## Types / Variants

**Apache Kafka.** *Model:* partitioned, append-only log; dumb broker, smart consumer;
pull. *Ordering:* total within a partition (key→partition). *Delivery:* at-least-once
by default; exactly-once *within Kafka* via idempotent producer + transactions. *
Throughput:* the throughput king — millions of msgs/s, sequential disk I/O,
zero-copy. *Retention/replay:* first-class — time/size retention plus **log
compaction**; replay by resetting offsets. *Ops:* heavy (brokers, partitions,
rebalancing; KRaft removes ZooKeeper). *Sweet spot:* high-volume event streaming,
event sourcing, CDC, multiple independent consumer groups, replay. *Not for:* simple
task queues, per-message TTL/priority/delay, tiny low-volume apps.

**RabbitMQ.** *Model:* smart broker — AMQP exchanges/bindings route to queues; push;
delete on ack. *Ordering:* per-queue, but breaks with competing consumers/requeues;
quorum/stream queues improve durability. *Delivery:* at-least-once with publisher
confirms + consumer acks; at-most-once if you skip acks. *Throughput:* tens of
thousands/s per queue — solid, well below Kafka. *Retention/replay:* none by default
(messages vanish on ack); the newer **stream** queue type adds a replayable log.
*Ops:* moderate; clustering and mirrored/quorum queues need care. *Sweet spot:*
complex routing, per-message priorities/TTL/delay, request/reply, task queues, RPC.
*Not for:* replay, very high throughput, retaining history.

**AWS SQS / SNS.** *Model:* SQS = managed queue; SNS = managed pub/sub; the classic
pattern is **SNS→SQS fan-out** (durable per-subscriber). *Ordering:* SQS Standard =
best-effort (and possible duplicates); **SQS FIFO** = strict ordering + dedup within a
message group, at lower throughput. *Delivery:* Standard = at-least-once; FIFO =
exactly-once *processing* via dedup IDs. *Throughput:* Standard = effectively
unlimited and auto-scaling; FIFO = capped (300–3000 msg/s with batching). *
Retention/replay:* up to 14 days retention but **no replay** (consumed = deleted; not
a log). *Ops:* near zero — fully managed. *Sweet spot:* AWS-native decoupling and task
queues with no infra to run. *Not for:* replay, streaming analytics, multi-cloud,
strict ordering at high throughput.

**NATS (+ JetStream).** *Model:* NATS core = blazing-fast, transient pub/sub (and
request/reply), no persistence. **JetStream** adds a durable, replayable **log** with
streams, consumers (push *and* pull), and at-least-once / exactly-once dedup.
*Ordering:* per-stream/subject sequence. *Delivery:* core = at-most-once (fire and
forget); JetStream = at-least-once + dedup window for effectively-once. *Throughput:*
very high, very low latency; single small binary. *Retention/replay:* JetStream
retains and replays (limits, interest, or work-queue policies). *Ops:* the lightest —
one Go binary, trivial to run, good at edge/IoT. *Sweet spot:* low-latency
microservice messaging, edge/IoT, request/reply, and lightweight durable streaming
without Kafka's weight. *Not for:* the deep Kafka ecosystem (Connect, ksqlDB, huge
retention) when you truly need it.

**Apache Pulsar.** *Model:* log like Kafka but with **compute/storage separation** —
stateless brokers over **BookKeeper** storage; natively does *both* streaming (log)
**and** queuing (shared subscriptions). *Ordering:* per-partition; subscription modes
(exclusive/failover/shared/key-shared) trade ordering for parallelism. *Delivery:*
at-least-once; exactly-once via transactions; per-message acks (unlike Kafka's
offset). *Throughput:* Kafka-class, with easier scaling/rebalancing thanks to
storage separation and **tiered storage** for cheap infinite retention. *Ops:* the
heaviest — brokers *plus* BookKeeper *plus* ZooKeeper/metadata; powerful but a lot to
run. *Sweet spot:* you want Kafka-grade streaming *and* queue semantics, strong
multi-tenancy, geo-replication, and long/tiered retention in one system. *Not for:*
teams wanting minimal operational surface.

**Redis Streams.** *Model:* an append-only **log** data type inside Redis (`XADD`,
`XREADGROUP`) with consumer groups and per-consumer pending lists. *Ordering:* total
within a stream (single shard). *Delivery:* at-least-once with explicit `XACK`;
pending-entries list enables claim/retry. *Throughput:* very high for in-memory
workloads; bounded by Redis memory and single-shard nature. *Retention/replay:*
capped by `MAXLEN`/`MINID` (trim) and by available memory; replay within retained
range; persistence via RDB/AOF. *Ops:* low *if you already run Redis*; durability is
weaker than disk-logs (async replication can lose recent writes). *Sweet spot:*
teams already on Redis wanting a lightweight stream/queue without a new system; modest
retention. *Not for:* large durable history, primary system-of-record streaming.

**Google Cloud Pub/Sub.** *Model:* fully-managed, global **pub/sub** with durable
per-subscription delivery; push *and* pull. *Ordering:* unordered by default;
**ordering keys** give per-key ordering (lower throughput). *Delivery:* at-least-once;
**exactly-once delivery** is supported per-subscription. *Throughput:* effectively
unlimited, auto-scaling, multi-region. *Retention/replay:* configurable retention +
**seek/snapshot** for replay (a managed log-like capability). *Ops:* near zero —
serverless. *Sweet spot:* GCP-native event ingestion and fan-out at any scale without
running infra. *Not for:* multi-cloud independence, the Kafka ecosystem, fine-grained
self-managed control.

## When to use / When NOT

- **Kafka — pick when:** high-throughput streaming, replay, event sourcing/CDC, and
  multiple consumer groups, and you (or a managed service) can operate it. **Skip
  when:** you want a simple task queue with priorities/TTL/delay, or volume is tiny.
- **RabbitMQ — pick when:** you need rich routing, per-message priority/TTL/delay,
  request/reply, and reliable task queues at moderate volume. **Skip when:** you need
  replay or Kafka-scale throughput.
- **SQS/SNS — pick when:** you're on AWS and want zero-ops decoupling and fan-out
  (SNS→SQS). **Skip when:** you need replay, streaming analytics, or multi-cloud.
- **NATS/JetStream — pick when:** you want ultra-low-latency microservice messaging,
  edge/IoT, or lightweight durable streaming without Kafka's weight. **Skip when:**
  you need Kafka's mature ecosystem and very large retention.
- **Pulsar — pick when:** you want streaming *and* queuing, multi-tenancy,
  geo-replication, and tiered long retention in one platform. **Skip when:** you can't
  absorb the heaviest operational footprint of the field.
- **Redis Streams — pick when:** you already run Redis and want a lightweight
  stream/queue with consumer groups and modest retention. **Skip when:** you need
  durable large-scale history or strong durability guarantees.
- **Google Pub/Sub — pick when:** you're on GCP and want serverless, global,
  auto-scaling pub/sub with replay. **Skip when:** you need multi-cloud or Kafka's
  ecosystem.

## Tradeoffs

The core tension: **Kafka/Pulsar** maximize throughput, replay, and consumer fan-out
at high ops cost; **RabbitMQ** maximizes routing/queue features at moderate scale;
**managed cloud (SQS/SNS, Pub/Sub)** minimize ops at the price of lock-in and limited
control; **NATS/Redis Streams** minimize footprint at the price of ecosystem and
durability depth. There is no "best" — only best-for-your-constraints.

| Broker | Model | Ordering | Delivery | Throughput | Retention/replay | Ops cost |
| ------ | ----- | -------- | -------- | ---------- | ---------------- | -------- |
| Kafka | Partitioned log (pull) | Per partition | At-least-once; EOS in-Kafka | Very high | First-class + compaction | High |
| RabbitMQ | Smart broker / queue (push) | Per queue (fragile) | At-least/at-most-once | Moderate | None (streams: yes) | Moderate |
| SQS / SNS | Managed queue / pub-sub | FIFO opt; Std best-effort | At-least-once; FIFO dedup | Std: vast; FIFO: capped | Retention, no replay | Near zero |
| NATS + JetStream | Pub/sub + durable log | Per subject/stream | Core: at-most; JS: at-least + dedup | Very high, low latency | JetStream replay | Lowest |
| Pulsar | Log + queue (BookKeeper) | Per partition | At-least-once; txn EOS | Very high | First-class + tiered | Highest |
| Redis Streams | In-memory log | Total (single shard) | At-least-once (XACK) | High (memory-bound) | Trim-bounded | Low (if on Redis) |
| Google Pub/Sub | Managed pub/sub | Keys opt; else none | At-least-once; EOD opt | Vast, global | Seek/snapshot replay | Near zero |

## Real-world examples

- **Kafka:** LinkedIn (its birthplace), Uber, Netflix, and most large streaming/CDC
  pipelines; the de facto standard for high-volume event streaming.
- **RabbitMQ:** widely used behind Celery/Sidekiq-style task systems, microservice
  RPC, and anywhere routing flexibility beats raw throughput.
- **SQS/SNS:** the default decoupling primitives across AWS-native architectures;
  SNS→SQS fan-out is a textbook pattern.
- **NATS/JetStream:** edge, IoT, and latency-sensitive microservice meshes; Synadia
  and CNCF back it.
- **Pulsar:** Yahoo (its origin), Tencent, and shops wanting unified streaming +
  queuing with multi-tenancy and geo-replication.
- **Redis Streams:** teams already on Redis adding event processing without a new
  cluster.
- **Google Pub/Sub:** GCP-native ingestion feeding Dataflow/BigQuery pipelines.
